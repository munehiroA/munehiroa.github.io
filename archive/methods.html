Measuring & controlling electrical states

How bacterial membrane potential is measured and perturbed using fluorescence, electrical stimulation and optical tools.

Measurement Electrical inputs Optical inputs Applications

What does a measurement tell us?

First distinguish absolute voltage, a relative change in one cell, and a difference between populations. These are different measurement goals. A fluorescent signal is not automatically a calibrated value in millivolts.

Time-lapse fluorescence microscopy
Follows individual cells over time. Interpretation depends on probe behaviour, illumination and image analysis.
Flow cytometry
Measures distributions across a population. A snapshot does not track the history of the same individual cell.
Electrical recording
Can reveal membrane and channel properties in suitable preparations. Small cell size and preparation requirements constrain experiments.

Controls matter

Signals can change with dye uptake, cell size, permeability, binding or light exposure. Interpret fluorescence alongside controls and an independent physiological readout. “Brighter” has no universal electrical meaning across probes.

Reveal a response through perturbation

Combining electrical stimulation and microscopy lets us compare cells in different physiological states. Our 2019 study connected these dynamics to proliferation capacity; a subsequent Bio-protocol paper describes the experimental method.

PNAS · 2019 Published protocol

Optical control of bacterial membrane potential

Light can deliver a perturbation at a chosen place and time. Our collaborative work used the membrane-targeted azobenzene Ziapin2 to modulate membrane potential in B. subtilis. Chemical photoswitches differ from genetically encoded light-sensitive proteins in their delivery and cellular interactions.

The 2023 result establishes optical modulation in a defined system. Other organisms and conditions require their own validation, including controls for the light and measurement probe.

Advanced Science · 2023

From a readout to an application

Engineering biology needs reliable relationships between an input, a cellular state and a useful output. BeAST explored electrical responses as a readout for antifungal screening in S. cerevisiae. It extends the question beyond bacteria while keeping the organism and validation scope explicit.
