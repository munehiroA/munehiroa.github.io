Bacterial electrophysiology

We ask how a bacterial cell’s electrical states influence its physiology and its response to external stimulus. Our experiments combine membrane-potential imaging, perturbation and quantitative models.

From our research

The same input, a different response

Our 2019 study linked electrically induced membrane-potential responses to proliferation capacity in B. subtilis and E. coli under the tested conditions. This study led to the creation of Cytecom Ltd.

PNAS · 2019

Emergence

How does local interaction create organisation at a larger scale? We investigate biofilms and swarms through their electrical, mechanical and metabolic interactions.

From our research

From swarming to biofilm formation

Our 2021 eLife paper investigated local transitions within swarming populations during stress-induced biofilm formation.

Engineering biology & tools

We develop tools that connect cellular state with biological function. Our engineering goal is to make these relationships more predictable across experimental contexts.

From our research

Optical modulation of membrane potential

We used the membrane-targeted photoswitch Ziapin2 to induce electrical responses in B. subtilis. It provides an experimental route to optical perturbation.

Advanced Science · 2023
From our research

BeAST: antifungal screening in yeast

The 2024 mBio study explored electrical response dynamics as a readout of growth inhibition in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. This is a model-system screening study, not a clinically validated test.

mBio · 2024

Physics of Life

Across these projects, we ask how energetics, signalling and physical interaction shape living systems. Bacteria provide a way to test broad physical ideas through quantitative experiments. Understanding, measurement and engineering inform one another.
